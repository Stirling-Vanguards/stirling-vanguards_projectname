# What Earth Cannot Replicate (Planetary Analog Limits)

**Status:** Draft reference note (shared environmental constraints across all terrestrial analog sites)

Even when a terrestrial field site closely matches lunar or Martian surface topography, fundamental planetary differences constrain how hardware and crew tests translate to flight operations:

1. **Reduced Surface Gravity:** Earth's gravitational acceleration (.0g$) cannot replicate lunar gravity ($\approx 0.165g$) or Martian gravity ($\approx 0.38g$). Wheel sinkage, rover traction, regolith ejection trajectories, and drill anchoring forces behave differently in partial gravity and require analytical or offload compensation.
2. **Atmospheric Pressure and Composition:** The Moon operates in ultra-high vacuum, while Mars has a thin $\text{CO}_2$-dominated atmosphere ($\approx 6\text{–}7\text{ mbar}$ average surface pressure, less than \%$ of Earth sea-level pressure). Terrestrial aerodynamic drag, convective cooling, acoustic propagation, and wind-driven dust transport do not match lunar or Martian surface conditions.
3. **Surface Radiation Environment:** Earth's geomagnetic field and dense atmosphere shield surface hardware from galactic cosmic rays (GCRs) and solar particle events (SPEs). Terrestrial field testing cannot validate long-duration electronics radiation tolerance or crew shielding without dedicated accelerator facilities.
4. **Lunar Polar Illumination and Cold Traps:** Near the lunar south pole, low solar elevation angles cast long, persistent shadows, creating permanently shadowed regions (PSRs) with cryogenic temperatures below \text{ K}$ alongside sunlit crater rims. Terrestrial polar sites experience seasonal day/night cycles and atmospheric heat transport that prevent true lunar cold-trap thermal behavior.
5. **Electrostatic and Abrasive Regolith Mechanics:** Lunar regolith consists of unweathered, jagged glass and mineral shards charged by solar wind plasma, without terrestrial moisture or atmospheric rounding to soften grain edges.
